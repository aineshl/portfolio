ShopSmart

A personal project, built solo. 50+ user surveys identified a real gap in how online marketplaces handle product comparison. I designed and shipped an AI-powered tool to solve it, from first survey to deployed product.

The Problem

Marketplaces have filters. They do not have synthesis. A 4.3-star product with 40 reviews and a 4.3-star product with 14,000 reviews look identical, but they are completely different buying propositions. Users were opening 6 tabs to make a decision that should take 30 seconds.

50+ surveys confirmed it. 72% spent too much time comparing products, 54% could not reliably interpret rating against review volume, and 44% said they spent too long reading reviews.

What I Built

Watch the product demo: ShopSmart demo video →

Two product URLs in. One clear recommendation out. The tool gives users what filters cannot: a verdict.

  • ShopSmart Score. A weighted algorithm combining price, rating, and review volume as a trust signal. It measures the reliability of stars, not just the stars themselves.
  • AI review summary. A BART model summarizing the 100 most recent reviews. Recency-weighted, not cherry-picked.
  • Parallel scraping. Both products processed simultaneously, with a progress state that communicates what is happening.

What Testers Told Me, and What I Did

15+ testers across 3 major iterations. Each round surfaced a clear signal.

Slow processing. Rebuilt to parallel scraping with a dynamic loading state. Perceived wait dropped significantly.

Opaque scores. Added an explanation of the scoring weights. Users do not trust numbers they cannot interrogate.

No recommendation. Added a recommended pick with written rationale. The whole point of the tool is to make a call.

Visual credibility. A full redesign pass. When you are asking someone to trust an algorithm with a purchase decision, aesthetics are a trust signal.

Results

Shipped a working product, tested it with real users, and kept improving it based on what they told me. Every decision traces back to something from the research: the scoring algorithm, the recommendation feature, the redesign. The surveys came first, the product followed.
