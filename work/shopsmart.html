The Problem

Amazon has filters. It doesn't have synthesis. A 4.3-star product with 40 reviews and a 4.3-star product with 14,000 reviews look identical, but they're completely different buying propositions. Users were opening 6 tabs to make a decision that should take 30 seconds.

50+ surveys confirmed it: 72% spent too much time comparing products, 54% couldn't reliably interpret rating vs. review volume, and 44% said they spent too long reading reviews.

What I Built

Two Amazon URLs in. One clear recommendation out. The tool gives users what filters can't: a verdict.

Watch the product demo: ShopSmart Demo Video →

  • ShopSmart Score — weighted algorithm combining price, rating, and review volume as a trust signal. Measures the reliability of stars, not just the stars themselves.
  • AI review summary — BART model summarizing the 100 most recent reviews. Recency-weighted, not cherry-picked.
  • Parallel scraping — both products processed simultaneously, with a progress state that communicates what's happening.

What Testers Told Me — And What I Did

15+ testers across 3 major iterations. Each round surfaced a clear signal:

Slow processing → Rebuilt to parallel scraping and a dynamic loading state. Perceived wait dropped significantly.

Opaque scores → Added an explanation of the scoring weights. Users don't trust numbers they can't interrogate.

No recommendation → Added a recommended pick with written rationale. The whole point of the tool is to make a call.

Visual credibility → Full redesign pass. When you're asking someone to trust an algorithm with a purchase decision, aesthetics are a trust signal.

Results

Shipped a working product, tested it with real users, and kept improving it based on what they told me. Every decision traces back to something from the research, the scoring algorithm, the recommendation feature, the redesign. The surveys came first, the product followed.
