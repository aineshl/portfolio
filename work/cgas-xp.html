The Problem

The existing explosion-proof offering ran on a white-label product. The structural problem: we couldn't compete on price, we couldn't differentiate on features, and we had no supply chain control. When the supplier was acquired by a competitor, lead times slowed and the entire product line became a business risk. The answer wasn't to find a better white-label. It was to build something we owned that was better suited for the market.

Discovery First

Before defining a single feature, I ran 10-15 customer interviews across three market segments, including field technicians who would install and maintain the product. I also did competitive research, reviewed industry standards, and mapped best practices in the hazardous location space.

The key insight: customers weren't just spec-ing explosion-proof for hazard classification. They wanted something that fit into existing systems without gateways or extra wiring, that technicians could service without re-entering the hazardous area, and that simplified system design overall. That shaped everything about how we built and positioned the CGAS-XP.

Other strong signals from discovery: remote calibration was considered mandatory by every participant; native BACnet was a genuine differentiator (virtually no competitor in our segment offered it); Bluetooth and cloud connectivity got consistent negative signals — both deliberately excluded from V1.

Product Design

I designed a platform, not a single SKU — built to support multiple configurations and expand into adjacent markets:

  • Up to 3 sensors (internal and/or remote) — reduces install cost, simplifies system design
  • Broad gas coverage: EC, IR, CAT, PID — wider than most competitors in the segment
  • Native BACnet and Modbus — a genuine differentiator at this price point
  • Remote calibration — validated as non-negotiable across all discovery participants
  • Built on existing firmware platform — simplifies service, training, and support from day one

A Key Tradeoff: Scoping Certification

After researching three certification labs and completing discovery, I cut ATEX certification from V1. Our primary market is North America, where UL certification is what customers actually require. ATEX would have added significant cost, 14+ weeks, and ongoing ISO audit obligations for minimal demand uplift in our target segment. The call protected the timeline, preserved budget for GTM investment, and let us build pipeline while certification was underway. The product architecture was designed with ATEX in mind for V2.

Business Case & Pricing

I built a full NPV model to evaluate in-house vs. white-label across multiple volume and pricing scenarios. The numbers made the decision clear:

  • In-house manufacturing achieved ~71% per-unit cost reduction, restoring pricing competitiveness and margin control
  • Customer price reduced ~40% vs. the white-label
  • Program NPV-positive within one year based on pre-launch order volume

Go-to-Market

We took a tiered approach to GTM, focusing on early wins first to generate revenue and build case studies before expanding into new markets:

  • Tier 1 — fast wins: Existing dealer network, established relationships, near-term pipeline (distilleries, fleet, boiler rooms)
  • Tier 2 — strategic expansion: Wastewater, chemical processing, midstream — longer cycles, new dealer development required
  • Tier 3 — long-term: Offshore and premium industrial; requires ATEX (planned for V2)

This included a trade show launch at AHR Expo, dealer briefing materials, sales training, and full technical documentation. I also attended two industry conferences outside our typical channels to do market research and gather insights that helped refine our positioning and better equip the sales team.

Results

  • Secured pre-launch orders matching the prior year's full white-label sales volume, before the product shipped
  • Eliminated a high-risk white-label dependency, the company now owns the platform, the pricing, and the roadmap
  • ~71% per-unit cost reduction achieved through in-house design and manufacturing
  • Entered CSA/UL certification with validated demand and early pipeline already established
  • First explosion-proof gas detector in our segment with native BACnet MS/TP communication, eliminating the need for gateways or protocol converters
